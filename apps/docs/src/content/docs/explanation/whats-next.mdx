Gambi is deliberately narrow. This page says what it does today, what it leaves to you, and where the work above it happens.

## What Gambi handles today

- A hub that holds rooms and the participants in them.
- Participants that share an OpenAI-compatible model or a coding harness (Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode), each through a tunnel its owner opened.
- Routing of inference calls by participant id, `model:<name>`, or `*`.
- Harness sessions relayed over ACP v1, with workspace artifacts sent back to the attached app.
- Live room events with timing and token counts for every request.

## What is still your code

Gambi moves work between participants. It does not decide how that work is organized:

- who talks when, and in what order
- what context participants share, and who keeps track of it
- how a task is split across harnesses, and who reviews the result

The [patterns guide](/guides/patterns/) shows how to write these on top of the SDK today.

## The layer above

Turn-taking, shared context, and orchestration across harnesses are being explored. Nothing there is promised, and none of it has a fixed shape yet.

One case where that exploration happens is [`gambiarra-lab`](https://github.com/arthurbm/gambiarra-lab), where the Gambiarra Club keeps the experiments from its in-person events, such as an event board and an orchestrator for harnesses. The lab consumes `gambi` and `gambi-sdk` from the outside, like any other app:

- Gambi never imports anything from the lab.
- Nothing from the lab is published while it has a single consumer.
- An idea moves closer to Gambi only after two separate experiences need it.

## What stays out of the hub

Whatever the layer above becomes, the hub stays a transport. Scheduling, workflow graphs, shared memory, task delegation, and agent identity do not go into it. Keeping them out is what lets plain OpenAI-compatible tools keep working against a room.
